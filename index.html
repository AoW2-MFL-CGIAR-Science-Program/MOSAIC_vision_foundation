About this document

This is a foundational document. Its purpose is to put in writing the shared vision for MOSAIC — what it is, why it exists, the value it adds, who it serves, and the principles that will guide how we build it — so the people leading this work can confirm we are all building toward the same picture before we commit to design and delivery.

It is written at the level of vision and principles rather than technical specification. The detailed technical design already exists in a companion document, the MFL Living Landscapes Geospatial Data Hub Proposal. This document sits above that one: it is the reference we return to whenever we need to remember what MOSAIC is for and what makes it worth building.

We ask readers to review it through one lens: does this reflect the same vision you hold? The five validation questions at the end of this document make that ask concrete — and you can record your responses inline as you read.

Section 01

What MOSAIC is

MOSAIC is the shared spatial intelligence network for the CGIAR MFL Science Programme. The name reflects the work: a mosaic is many distinct pieces forming one coherent picture — exactly what this initiative does, bringing together geospatial data and capacity from across ten CGIAR centres into a unified view of our Living Landscapes.

Where the name comes from

The name works on two levels. As an image, a mosaic assembles many separate tiles into one coherent picture. As an acronym, its letters spell out exactly what the initiative does — the full name is Multifunctional Open Spatial Analysis & Integration Collaborative.

Across the programme there is already a great deal of geospatial work underway. What is missing is the connective tissue: a common way to know what exists, to reach trusted external data without each team rebuilding the same pipelines, and to combine global and local layers into something coherent at the landscape scale. MOSAIC is that connective tissue — best understood as three things working together.

A catalogue

that makes the programme's geospatial assets discoverable, well-described and quality-tracked.

Connectors & frameworks

that give researchers reliable, documented access to the major external data services the world already operates.

A coordination function

that gives coherence to what the different centres and partners are already doing, so effort is shared rather than duplicated.

A note on language: MOSAIC is deliberately framed as a coordination network and metadata system, not as a monolithic store that holds a copy of everything. We may casually call it a data hub, but the heart of it is connection and coherence, not accumulation — and that is what keeps it lightweight, low-cost, and realistic to sustain.

What MOSAIC is not

It is not an attempt to re-host the world's geospatial data — we connect to Google Earth Engine, CHIRPS, and other services; we do not duplicate them. It is not a single centre's product imposed on the others. 

Section 02

Why MOSAIC, and why now

The programme needs spatially explicit evidence to characterize ecosystem condition, find where landscapes are under stress, and prioritize where action matters most. That evidence is only useful if it can be found, trusted, and combined across themes and landscapes. Today it cannot — for four connected reasons.

No common catalogue

No one can say with confidence what spatial data exists across the ten centres, so the same global datasets are re-acquired while unique local data stays invisible.

No agreed landscape boundaries

Without an agreed extent for each Living Landscape, data cannot be organized, compared, or integrated by landscape.

No shared minimum standards

Data may arrive without a coordinate reference system, temporal coverage, or a license, so users cannot judge whether it is fit for purpose.

No path from "exists" to "ready to use"

Listing a dataset in a spreadsheet is not the same as making it usable. The programme needs a visible route to validated, analysis-ready layers.

Section 03

Vision and mission

Vision

Every team working on a Living Landscape can find, trust, and combine the spatial evidence they need — drawing on the best global data the world maintains and the local data only CGIAR holds — to understand landscape condition and prioritize action.

Mission

To coordinate and give coherence to the geospatial work already happening across CGIAR centres and institutions— providing a shared catalogue, common standards, and reliable connections to external and local data — so the programme can move from fragmented effort to a unified landscape view.

"Be part of the shared spatial intelligence network for multifunctional living landscapes."

The idea at the centre of MOSAIC
Section 04

Our distinctive value: connect, don't duplicate

The world already has organizations that build and operate enormous geospatial data services far better than any single programme could — Google Earth Engine, CHIRPS, ESA WorldCover, SRTM, JRC Global Surface Water. They are world-class, well-funded, continuously updated, and freely accessible. It would make no sense for MOSAIC to copy them.

MOSAIC's value is not in holding this data. It is in making it usable for our specific landscapes, and in combining it with the local data that we have.

1
Frameworks and connections to reach external data

Rather than every researcher learning how to pull, clip and reproject a CHIRPS time series or an Earth Engine layer for the Lake Victoria Basin, MOSAIC provides documented, reusable connections. The hard, repetitive plumbing is solved once and shared, with provenance and parameters recorded so results are reproducible.

2
The local data the global services don't have

Field-level soil samples, locally validated degradation maps, agrobiodiversity records, and socio-economic survey layers — built by CGIAR centres and partners over the years. This is precisely what turns a generic global map into a decision-relevant landscape assessment. MOSAIC catalogues it, applies minimum quality standards, and makes it discoverable alongside the global layers.

3
Combining the two into a coherent landscape view

A global land-cover product becomes far more powerful overlaid with local degradation data, water stress, and socio-economic vulnerability for the same area, within an agreed boundary. That combination — global reach plus local depth, organized by landscape — is what MOSAIC uniquely enables, and what the Digital Twin and participatory planning work need.

IN SHORT:

The global providers give us breadth, our centres give us depth, and MOSAIC is the layer that connects them and keeps them coherent.

Section 05

Guiding principles

These describe how we make decisions as MOSAIC is built. When a trade-off appears, we resolve it in favour of these.

Connect before collecting
Where data is already well maintained elsewhere, we link to it rather than re-host it. We hold and curate data only where we add genuine value — primarily local datasets and analysis-ready landscape layers.
Start simple, grow with evidence
We begin with the lightest tools that work and add complexity only when real use demands it. A focused, working catalogue beats an ambitious platform that never launches.
Coordinate, don't command
MOSAIC gives coherence to existing work; it does not gatekeep or override centres. Centres are contributors and partners, and their existing work is acknowledged, not absorbed.
Open by default
Programme outputs and global public datasets are openly accessible. Access is restricted only where licensing, sensitivity, or ownership genuinely require it — and even then, the data stays discoverable through its metadata.
Quality is visible, not assumed
Every dataset carries clear metadata and a stated readiness level, so users always know what they are getting and whether it is fit for their purpose.
FAIR and interoperable
We follow recognized standards (such as STAC for spatial metadata) so MOSAIC can feed and be fed by wider CGIAR platforms rather than becoming an island, and we adopt cloud-optimized formats (e.g. COG, Zarr, GeoParquet) and metadata standards shared with sibling initiatives such as the Climate Data Hub and FairGrounds, so our catalogue interoperates with theirs rather than diverging.
Ready for machines as well as people
Data is machine-readable and API-accessible by design, so it can power partners' own analyses and AI-assisted workflows — our value is trustworthy, well-described data, not being the only ones who can build on it.
Built to be sustained
We favour low-cost, low-maintenance, well-documented solutions a small team can realistically keep running — because a system no one can maintain helps no one.
Section 06

What MOSAIC delivers in 2026

The vision is multi-year, but this year's commitments are deliberately focused. For the priority living landscapes  the 2026 deliverables are:

Landscape delineation maps

Clear, agreed spatial boundaries for each priority Living Landscape. No centre has produced these yet — they are the foundation everything else organizes around, and the first concrete action. Where robust hydrological units (basins and catchments) already exist, we start from them rather than drawing boundaries from scratch, reconciling them with administrative and ecological extents.

A gap analysis

Per country, an inventory of what geospatial datasets exist across centres, what can be filled in 2026, and what waits until 2027 — organized by the 13 MFL themes so gaps are visible per theme and per landscape.

A minimum spatial package

A defined, agreed set of layers for a baseline assessment of each landscape — land cover and use, ecosystem condition, land health, water, biodiversity, socio-economic — so every landscape is described comparably.

Together these feed directly into the Digital Twin and Vision-to-Action participatory planning workstreams of AoW2. Explicitly out of scope for 2026 is a fully harmonized, centralized data repository — a longer-term goal we are deliberately not promising this year.

Section 07

Who MOSAIC serves

MOSAIC is intended, in its mature form, as an open resource for the wider landscapes community — a global public good. Getting there responsibly means serving one audience well before we open to the next. We therefore build first for the CGIAR research community, not because the ambition is narrow, but because that is how we earn the right to serve a wider audience with data people can trust.

Phase 1 · Now
The CGIAR research community

Researchers, geospatial focal points and analysts inside CGIAR centres working on the Living Landscapes. They are the contributors and first beneficiaries — registering and discovering datasets, reaching external data through shared connectors, and drawing on the minimum spatial package. Serving this group well is the priority: they populate and validate MOSAIC and feed the Digital Twin and planning work.

→
Phase 2 · Forward-looking
Partners and local governments

As the foundations mature, the intent is to open relevant, appropriately licensed parts of MOSAIC to external partners and to local and national governments in our landscapes. For them, value lies in transparent, well-documented landscape evidence for planning — data trustworthy enough to power their own tools and analyses, not only interfaces we build — with clear attribution to contributing centres. This wider opening is sequenced after the internal foundations are solid — not a Phase 1 promise.

Designing for the CGIAR community first, with the partner and government audience explicitly in mind, shapes choices we make now: open-by-default access, recognized standards, clear attribution, and visible quality. These are what make later opening credible rather than a scramble.

Section 08

How MOSAIC works, in plain terms

MOSAIC separates three concerns so we never have to wait for full infrastructure before delivering value:

01
Knowing what exists

A catalogue recording each dataset's description, location, owner, coverage, license and quality status.

02
Reaching the data

Connectors to external services for global layers, and curated storage for local datasets and analysis-ready products we hold ourselves, published in cloud-optimized formats.

03
Serving it

Making data discoverable and accessible to users and, in time, to applications like the Digital Twin through standard protocols.

Crucially, every dataset carries a visible readiness level so users always know what they are looking at — preventing the common confusion between "a dataset is listed" and "a dataset is ready to use":

The approach is phased on purpose. We start with the simplest tools that work — a structured catalogue, shared vocabularies, documentation, and a small set of quality checks — and add capability only as real use justifies it. This keeps cost low (effectively zero infrastructure cost to begin) and within what a small coordinating team can sustain.

Section 09

How MOSAIC fits with other platforms

MOSAIC is designed to complement, not compete with, the other data efforts in and around the programme. Being explicit about these boundaries avoids duplication and turf confusion.

Climate Data Hub
Covers climate layers; MOSAIC covers the non-climate spatial themes and links to it for climate data through metadata, rather than re-storing it.
GARDIAN / Fairgrounds & CGSpace / Dataverse
CGIAR-wide platforms for discovery and deposit; MOSAIC is a domain-specific feeder whose metadata is designed to be harvestable by them, with finished datasets deposited there and cross-linked.
Digital Twin & Vision-to-Action
MOSAIC's primary internal customers: it supplies the validated, organized landscape layers they consume.

The throughline is consistent with our principles: MOSAIC connects to and supports the broader ecosystem rather than trying to replace any part of it. We will actively engage with parallel data efforts — the Climate Data Hub first — to align on shared infrastructure and standards, and we are open to a cross-programme coalition or community of practice (the CSI community is one possible route) so these hubs form one constellation rather than fragmenting.

Governance and roles

MOSAIC works because responsibilities are clear and lightweight.

Geospatial focal points

Scientific reviewers
From AoW2

Confirm thematic fitness for purpose when datasets reach validation.

Programme leadership
Christopher Kettle · Lulseged Desta

Sets direction, approves governance changes, and ensures alignment with MFL Science Program deliverables.

A lightweight protocol for scouting and evaluating newly emerging, relevant data layers — so the catalogue keeps pace with new global and local sources — sits with the focal points and the technical coordinator.

The coordinating role is explicitly one of giving coherence, not of producing every output single-handedly. MOSAIC succeeds when the whole AoW2 community contributes within a common framework — that is the design, and the mitigation against over-reliance on any one person.

Section 11

A high-level path forward

The detailed roadmap lives in the companion proposal; at the level of vision, the path has three movements — each independently valuable.

1
Establish the foundations this year

Agree the landscape boundaries for the Living Landscapes; stand up the shared catalogue and vocabularies; pre-populate known global datasets; run facilitated registration sessions with focal points; and produce the first gap analysis per landscape and theme.

2
Make it a working service

Add a simple route for centres to contribute data, apply quality checks, publish a searchable catalogue, and assemble the first minimum spatial package for a priority landscape.

3
Deepen and widen

Provide richer geospatial services for the Digital Twin, produce analysis-ready packages for the priority landscapes, extend the proven template to Colombia and the Mekong, and begin opening appropriate access to partners and governments.

Across these movements, the first pilot application of MOSAIC is the MFL living landscape Digital Twin — the first consumer of MOSAIC's organized, quality-tracked layers and the proving ground for the service.

If we only completed the first movement, the programme would still be meaningfully better off than it is today.

Section 12

What success looks like

We will know MOSAIC is working when a researcher new to a Living Landscape can, in one place, see what spatial data already exists for it, reach trusted global layers without rebuilding pipelines, find the local datasets only CGIAR holds, and understand at a glance what is ready to use.

We will know it is working when centres stop independently re-acquiring the same global datasets, when gaps are visible and being deliberately filled, and when the Digital Twin and planning teams are drawing on organized, quality-tracked layers rather than chasing scattered files.

And we will know the vision is maturing when partners and governments in our landscapes can draw on transparent, well-attributed evidence to inform their own decisions.
